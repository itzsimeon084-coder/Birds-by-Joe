Parrotlets

Parrotlets are among the smallest members of the parrot family and are popular as household companion birds.

Despite their small size, they are cheerful, active and territorial birds with strong personalities.

There are several attractive colors and varieties available for people interested in keeping a Parrotlet.

The Pacific Parrotlet and Green-Rumped Parrotlet are among the varieties commonly preferred by bird enthusiasts.

They are also relatively quiet compared with many larger parrot species.

Native Region/Natural Habitat

Parrotlets are naturally found throughout Mexico and Central and South America.

Their natural environments include forests, woodland areas and other tropical and subtropical habitats.

Physical Characteristics

Parrotlets are extremely small parrots.

Their small bodies make them suitable for people looking for a companion bird without the size of a larger parrot.

Adult Parrotlets can weigh approximately 33 grams, depending on the species and individual bird.

Their wingspan is approximately nine and a half inches.

They are available in a variety of bright colors, including blue, green and yellow.

Male and female birds can have different feather markings depending on the species.

Some females may display green coloration around the face and wings, while males of certain varieties can have blue markings.

Personality & Behavior

Do not let the small size of a Parrotlet fool you. These birds can have very large personalities.

They can be energetic, confident and highly territorial.

A Parrotlet may attempt to challenge other birds or household pets, so appropriate supervision is important.

They can also become attached to their owners and enjoy regular interaction.

Because they can become independent or territorial, owners should spend time socializing and interacting with them.

Hand-feeding and gentle interaction can help develop trust between the bird and its owner.

Parrotlets need exercise and enrichment to remain active and healthy.

Toys and supervised playtime can help prevent boredom.

Their lifespan can reach several decades when they receive appropriate care.

Speech & Sounds

Parrotlets are relatively quiet compared with many larger parrots.

They can learn to imitate a number of words, although they are not generally considered among the strongest talking parrots.

They can also produce pleasant songs, chirps and other vocalizations.

Their sounds can be used for communication, attracting a mate and establishing territory.

In addition to vocal sounds, Parrotlets may communicate through actions such as wing flapping, chewing and pecking.
