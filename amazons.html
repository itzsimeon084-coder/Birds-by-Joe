Blue-Cheeked Amazon

Distribution / Range

You will find the rare Blue-Cheeked Amazon species across South America, particularly northeastern regions.

Prominent areas include southeastern Venezuela, northeastern French Guiana, Suriname and northern Guyana.

Blue-Cheeked Amazons live near humid forests and subtropical cloud forests. They can also occur in savanna woodlands.

Seasonal movements towards coastal regions can occur when food availability changes.

The Blue-Cheeked Amazon is considered vulnerable because of habitat loss, food shortages and the pet trade.

Description

Blue-Cheeked Amazon parrots normally measure approximately 13 to 14 inches in length and weigh around 480 to 600 grams.

Their plumage is predominantly green, with blue coloration around the cheeks and yellow-orange coloration around the crown and wing areas.

Their bills are generally pink and red shaded with grey coloration.

Adults normally have red or orange irises surrounded by grey eye rings.

Blue-Fronted Amazon Parrot

Blue-Fronted Amazons average approximately 14 to 15 inches in length.

They generally weigh between approximately 275 and 510 grams, although individual birds can vary.

Blue-Fronted Amazons display a wide variety of facial and head coloration.

Some birds have turquoise-blue coloration across the forehead, while others can have blue coloration extending across the face, chest and neck.

Yellow markings may also appear around the head and face.

Individual birds can vary significantly in their facial pattern and the amount of yellow or red coloration around the shoulder.

Their beak is dark grey and their feet are grey.

Yellow-Headed Amazon

The Yellow-Headed Amazon averages approximately 15 to 17 inches in length.

When excited, the feathers around the neck and crown can rise, making the head appear considerably larger.

These Amazons have rounded wings and a square tail, giving them a strong physical build.

Their body is predominantly green with a yellow head and red coloration along parts of the wings.

Flight feathers can display bluish-violet and black shades, while hidden red patches may occur on the tail and flight feathers.

Both sexes look similar.

Yellow-Headed Amazons are highly intelligent, imaginative birds that can become very attached to people.

They are particularly well known for their talking and singing abilities.

Some individuals can become nippy, so they require appropriate handling, training and socialization.

They can also be noisy and require suitable toys, branches and activities to redirect their energy.

Yellow-Nape Amazon

  • Size: 12-15 inches
  • Average weight: 480-680 grams
  • Green forehead and crown with a yellow band across the lower nape and hind neck.
  • Some birds develop more yellow coloration as they mature.
  • Dark grey beak with a somewhat lighter upper-beak base.
  • Grey feet.

Yellow-Napes are loved for their intelligence, clown-like personality and impressive talking and singing abilities.

They are particularly known for their strong sense of pitch and remarkably human-sounding voice when talking.

A healthy Amazon parrot can live approximately 50 to 60 years or even longer with good nutrition and care.

Striking mutations of the Yellow-Nape have also occurred in aviaries.

Mealy Parrot

Mealy Parrots are popular for their social abilities and can live comfortably in pairs and large flocks.

They can also interact with other bird species, including macaws.

The Mealy Parrot normally measures around 15 to 17 inches, equivalent to approximately 38 to 41 cm.

Their weight can range from approximately 540 to 700 grams.

They are generally calm and less noisy than some other parrot species.

Their head can feature violet, blue and purple coloration while the body is predominantly green.

Yellow patches may appear on the head and the tail feathers are often lime green.

The crown can have purplish or bluish coloration.

The beak is normally yellowish and the unfeathered areas around the eyes are white with reddish-orange shades.

Orange-Winged Amazons

Orange-Winged Amazons are among the smaller Amazon parrots.

They average approximately 12 to 13 inches in length, although some subspecies can be as small as 10 inches.

They generally weigh around 340 grams.

They are sometimes confused with Blue-Fronted Amazons.

One of their distinguishing features is the orange coloration in their wings, which gives them their name.

Their plumage is mainly green with a blue forehead, yellow coloration around the crown, cheeks and outer tail tips, and an orange wing patch.

Their beak is horn-colored with a grey tip. Their feet are pale grey or horn-brown.

Males and females look alike.

Orange-Winged Amazons are strong chewers. They require appropriate training and supervision because they can chew materials that may be dangerous inside a home.

Proper discipline, training and appropriate chewing toys are important from a young age.
