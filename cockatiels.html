Cockatiels

Cockatiels are extremely comical and social birds that are popular as house pets.

They are amazing whistlers, which makes them pleasing for many pet owners.

Male cockatiels whistle serenades to their favorite people, especially owners.

Male cockatiels may even direct their serenades to their own mirror reflections or favorite objects.

If they are not whistling, you will surely find them snuggling on the arm or shoulder of their owner or favorite person.

They are good at looking for food in their cage and making it look interesting.

For pet-loving families in need of a social and affectionate pet, cockatiels are an ideal choice.

Native Region / Natural Habitat

Cockatiels don't carry a screech similar to the ear-piercing sound from parrots.

This is because they come from open environments like semi-arid and nearly arid regions across Australia.

That is the native region cockatiels belong to.

In the wilderness, cockatiels have to come down to the ground to search for food.

In the wild, cockatiels breed rapidly, but breeding them in captivity is also easy.

Easy breeding and wide availability are some of the main reasons why they are readily available in the market for pet owners.

It also lowers their cost, making them affordable pets for families.

Since cockatiels remain light sleepers throughout their life in the wild, they are always suspicious and remain on high alert for fear of predators.

This isn't the case with most captive pet cockatiels, but night-fright incidents once in a while are common.

If your pet cockatiel starts thrashing its head on the cage, you can leave a night light on to help them feel comfortable.

A female cockatiel can make a great companion.

Personality & Behavior

You can tell about a cockatiel's mood from the positioning of the feathers on their crest.

If the feathers are straight up, the bird is either extremely curious or startled by something.

When relaxing and calm, it will hold the crest feathers slightly backward while the cheek feathers will fluff up.

If the bird is calm and happy, it will keep grinding its beak.

If the bird is defensive, it will hiss and hold the crest feathers close to the head and flatten them.

Non-toxic and easy-breakage chew toys are suitable play objects for cockatiels.

Bead fiddles are hard-plastic toys among cockatiels' favorite toys.

Since they are amazing whistlers, they look for mirrors and reflective objects to whistle to.

Keep a close check on where your pet cockatiel is because they have a constant habit of moving down to the floor to play or forage for food.

Often, female cockatiels will look for secretive corners, but you can get them back by training them to whistle on cue.

Speech & Sounds

While these birds can speak a few phrases or words, male cockatiels whistle more than female cockatiels.

Their chirping sound is low, which makes it clear that they are suitable as apartment pets.

If a cockatiel sees you leaving the room, they may chirp at you if you are their favorite person and want to stay with you.

White Faced Cockatiel

As some of the most popular caged birds, the White-Faced Cockatiel is a member of the Cockatoo family.

New and inexperienced pet-lovers sometimes confuse the White-Faced Cockatiel with Albino Cockatiels.

These birds are highly social and love interacting with their owners.

Since they are easy to breed, they have quickly become among the most common pet birds in households, including apartments.

They can live with other birds from the same family, as well as some birds from other families, quite peacefully.

However, they don't dwell well in solitude, and keeping a fellow bird in their cage can help them feel safe when they are alone.

The signature feature of White-Faced Cockatiels is the result of a mutation in the Cockatoo family.

Their body is covered in patches of charcoal grey color.

They don't have orange patches or yellow shades on their cheeks either.

Unlike other Cockatiels from the same family, White-Faced Cockatiels have grey plumage.

Their genetic mutation prevents the appearance of yellow color pigments, which keeps their plumage grey-colored.

A White-Faced Cockatiel can live around 15-25 years in captivity with proper care.

Exercise is important for all cockatiels, especially this one.

An adult White-Faced Cockatiel can measure around 12-13 inches.

These Cockatiels weigh around 85-115 grams.

Lutino Cockatiel

Lutino Cockatiels are highly popular household pets because they are easy to breed and take care of.

Their distinct appearance makes them easy to recognize.

Similar to White-Faced Cockatiels, this breed is also popular for its unique mutation.

These birds tend to thrive in a joyous environment around playful toys and objects.

They are mild-tempered and easy to care for.

They need love, affection and attention from their owners.

The color combination of Lutino Cockatiels is one of the easiest to recognize.

Both males and females have similar color schemes.

Their bodies are generally white with a yellow-colored head and distinct rose-pink cheek patches.

They often have a high yellow head crest extending toward the back.

Lutino Cockatiels can also have pied markings, although these are relatively rare.

A more common name for Lutino Cockatiel is Lutino Tiel or Moonbeam Cockatiel.

Lutino Cockatiels can live around 16-25 years in captivity with proper care, nutrition and exercise.

They are normally around 12-13 inches long and weigh approximately 85-125 grams.

Albino Cockatiel

Although the name is Albino, these Cockatiels aren't technically albino in their plumage.

Some bird-lovers suggest the name White-Faced Lutino Cockatiel because these birds carry characteristics of both.

As social birds, they love staying near their owners almost all the time.

Providing them with a spacious cage can also be sufficient.

They may not talk as fluently or for as long as some other Cockatiels, but they serenade and whistle to show affection towards their owners.

Albino Cockatiels have a completely bold white appearance that is distinctive.

Their solid white body is accompanied by prominent red eyes.

Female Cockatiels may carry a color pattern called tail barring on the underside of the tail.

These birds are sometimes regarded as Clear Pied Cockatiels.

Unlike original Cockatoos that have markings on their wings, these birds have pure white wing feathers.

Albino Cockatiels can live around 15 years and sometimes longer with the right nutritional and exercise regime.

They require sufficient exercise and playtime regularly.

They measure around 12-13 inches.

They can weigh over 100 grams but usually remain in the 70-120 gram range.
