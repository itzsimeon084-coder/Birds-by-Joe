Budgies

You may know Budgies as a budgerigar, which is commonly used to refer to "parakeet," a generic term across the United States.

Budgies are quite small, measuring only about 6-7 inches in total.

Their Latin name roughly translates to "song-bird with wavy lines," which is an appropriate description of the species.

Budgies came to Europe in 1838 and quickly became one of the most common pet birds in European households.

Their calm chirping and bold green appearance made them popular, while their ability to breed quickly helped spread their popularity among households of different backgrounds.

In 1894, Australia banned the export of Budgies to Europe. European breeders therefore continued breeding the Budgies already present there.

Today, Budgies are among the most popular pet birds in the world.

Native Region / Natural Habitat

Budgies are native to Australia. Australian grasslands are their main natural habitat.

Wild Budgies are generally green and slightly smaller than many domestic birds found in pet shops today.

Their natural environment does not always provide ample water, so large flocks travel across Australian scrublands in search of water.

During rainy periods, when food and water are more plentiful, wild Budgies can breed rapidly.

Their breeding and residence can include tree limbs and hollow trees.

Personality & Behavior

If you are looking for a hands-on pet, Budgies can make excellent companions.

Some people enjoy watching them in cages or colony enclosures, while others prefer hand-taming and petting them.

Budgies are social birds and can become very friendly with their owners.

Because they are social, keeping a single isolated bird may not always be suitable.

Budgies are generally friendly around children, although adult supervision is important.

Speech & Sounds

From words and whistles to complete phrases, Budgies are among the better talking birds in the parrot family.

Male Budgies generally talk more easily and whistle frequently, while females can also vocalize but may do so less frequently.

English Budgies

English Budgies, also known as English Parakeets, are peaceful and relatively quiet birds with a loving disposition.

They tend to be affectionate towards their owners.

English Budgies developed through selective breeding in England.

Among the different types of Budgies, English Budgies are generally larger than American and Australian Budgies.

They are popular with families living in smaller homes and apartments because of their friendly nature.

Wild Australian Budgies originally had a yellow and green color combination.

Today, more than 30 color mutations can be found among English Budgies.

Compared with wild Budgies, English Budgies generally have fluffier and longer feathers. Their heads appear larger and they can have beard-like feathers around the neck.

Their traditional coloration includes yellow and green, with green bands across the body and a predominantly yellow head.

English Budgies generally weigh around 45-65 grams and can measure approximately 10 inches from head to the tip of the tail.

With proper diet, exercise and care, English Budgies can live for many years.

They require regular playtime and movement, making a suitably sized cage important, particularly when keeping them in groups.

Rainbow Budgie

There are different kinds of Budgies, including English Budgies.

Rainbow Budgies are recognized by their combination of different colors and characteristics.

Unlike Budgies with solid bands of color, Rainbow Budgies display softer colors that blend into one another.

Rainbow Budgies are associated with four different genetic mutations: Yellowface, Opaline, Blue-based and Clearwing.

Spangle Rainbow Budgies can have black-edged feathers with white or yellow-colored spots.

Their overall appearance can include combinations such as mauve, cobalt and sky-blue shades.

The wing tips can have a stronger blue tone while the upper wing area may display softer shades.

Their heads are generally similar in color to their bodies, particularly among blue-based birds.

Rainbow Budgies can be excellent companions and have the potential to become good talkers.

They are generally friendly and affectionate with their owners and can learn to mimic speech.
