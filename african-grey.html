African Grey Parrots

African Grey parrots are intelligent and highly popular companion birds. The original page features the two principal varieties: Timneh African Grey and Congo African Grey.

Timneh African Grey

The Timneh African Grey is one of the two principal African Grey varieties. It is generally smaller than the Congo variety.

Timneh Greys are known for their darker grey coloration. The upper chest, head and back can have particularly dark grey feathers, while the abdomen has contrasting lighter grey areas.

The head can show light scalloped markings, while the darker feathers may have subtle bluish tones.

The tail commonly displays maroon, dark red and brown coloration. The beak can contain pink and black coloring, with the lower mandible being black.

Timneh African Greys are smaller than Congo African Greys. The original page describes them as approximately 9 to 11 inches in overall body length, with a wingspan of roughly 13 to 15 inches and a weight of approximately 250g to 375g.

Congo African Grey

The Congo African Grey is another major variety of African Grey parrot and is generally larger than the Timneh.

One of its most noticeable characteristics is its bright red tail, which provides a strong contrast against its predominantly grey body.

Congo African Greys can also be associated with regional names such as Cameroon, Angola and Togo Greys.

Their body feathers range from dark grey through lighter silvery shades. Whitish edging can appear around the feathers, particularly toward the head and chest.

The wings and primary coverts are generally very dark, sometimes appearing almost black.

The eyes have prominent light skin patches. A phenomenon sometimes called "blushing" can cause these normally pale areas to take on a pinkish appearance.

The nails and toes are black, while the brilliant reddish tail is one of the most distinctive features of the Congo African Grey.
